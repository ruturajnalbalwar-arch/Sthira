**Subject:** Sit2Stand: citing your method, and a question about the published keypoints

Dear Dr. Boswell,

We are three students building Sthira, a browser app for HABIT Innovate 2026. It uses MediaPipe Pose to help a physiotherapist count the CDC 30-second chair stand and time two other CDC STEADI tests.

Your paper, "Smartphone videos of the sit-to-stand test predict osteoarthritis and health outcomes in a nationwide study" (npj Digital Medicine 6, 32, 2023), shaped our approach. We have written our own implementation of your idea of segmenting repetitions from the head's vertical path, normalised by body height. We did not copy code from your repository, and we cite the paper wherever we describe the method.

We would be grateful for your answers to two questions:

1. May we use the keypoints published in stanfordnmbl/sit2stand-analysis for a research comparison with our own pilot clips? We would not redistribute them, and we would cite the paper.
2. We could not find a licence file in the repository. Do you intend to apply one?

In return, we are happy to share what we find about loose clothing. Our pilot includes 30-second chair stands in saree, dhoti and lungi, which can hide the hips and knees. We will send you a short summary once we have it.

If you would rather we did not use the data, we will simply cite the paper. Thank you for publishing your work so openly.
